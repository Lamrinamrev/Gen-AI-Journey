# "Guard the Ghoda" — 15-Minute Chess Lesson

**Goal:** She walks away wanting to play "protect the Ghoda" one more time — not remembering a rule.

---

## Block 1: The Story Hook (5 min)

Set up just the board's back rank — nothing else. Point to one lone Ghoda sitting a few squares out, no other pieces near it.

Say something like:
*"Dekho, yeh Ghoda akela jungle mein khada hai. Koi uske paas nahi hai. Agar koi sher (dushman ka mohra) aaya, toh Ghoda ko bachane wala koi nahi."*

Then ask — don't tell:
**"Tumhe kya lagta hai, Ghoda ko safe feel karne ke liye kya chahiye?"**

Let her guess. Whatever she says ("dost," "guard," "koi aur mohra") — run with it, even if it's not chess-accurate yet. You're planting the *idea* of protection, not the definition.

---

## Block 2: Two Ghode, One Guard (5 min)

Now place a second piece (a Pyada/pawn works well) one square behind or beside the Ghoda — in real defending position.

Say:
*"Ab dekho — yeh Pyada Ghoda ke peeche khada hai. Agar sher Ghoda ko pakadne aaye, toh Pyada usko wapas pakad sakta hai."*

Then make it a game, not a lecture. Set up 2-3 tiny scenarios on the board (just 3-4 pieces each) and ask each time:

**"Yeh Ghoda guarded hai ya akela hai? Tumhe kaise pata chala?"**

Let her point and reason out loud. Wrong answers aren't corrected — just ask "kya tumhe aur dekhna hai?" and let her look again.

---

## Block 3: Play "Protect the Ghoda" (5 min)

Simple mini-game: place 4-5 pieces total on the board. Her job — move one piece per turn so that every Ghoda on the board has a "guard" nearby by the end.

Keep it playful:
*"Chalo dekhte hain — kya tum sab Ghodo ko surakshit rakh sakti ho is board pe?"*

If she succeeds, celebrate the *feeling*, not the rule: *"Dekho, ab koi Ghoda akela nahi hai!"*

End here — even if she wants to keep playing, that's your win condition. Stop while she still wants more.

---

**One line to remember for next session:** she's building the *instinct* of "who's alone right now?" — the vocabulary (defending, protecting) can come later once the instinct is there.
